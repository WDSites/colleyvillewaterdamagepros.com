Appliance Leak Water Damage in Colleyville, TX

Washing machines, dishwashers and refrigerator ice-maker lines cause some of the most overlooked water losses in a house. Many leak slowly behind or beneath the unit. For the room itself, see kitchen water damage.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

How household appliances end up leaking

Most homes in Colleyville have a washer, a dishwasher and a refrigerator with an ice maker or water dispenser. All three are tied into the plumbing by thin lines that age out of sight. When one fails, the water lands on cabinet bases, flooring or the subfloor, often while nobody is home.

The failures are not mysterious. They are small parts doing a pressurized job for years, and rubber, plastic and clamps do not last forever.

Where each appliance typically fails

ApplianceCommon failureHow fast it floods
Washing machineRubber supply hose splits or a fitting loosens at the valveFast, since the hose stays under line pressure
Washing machineDrain hose slips off the standpipe or the box overflowsFast during a spin cycle, slow if only a clog
DishwasherDoor gasket wears, inlet valve fails or a hose clamp loosensSlow to moderate, often during a cycle
RefrigeratorIce-maker supply line kinks, cracks or loses a compression fittingSlow and constant, often hidden behind the unit
RefrigeratorDefrost drain clogs and water spills inside or underneathSlow, shows up as a puddle or swollen floor edge

Braided steel hoses last longer than plain rubber, but they still wear and should be checked.

A washer hose that gives way is the classic fast loss. A refrigerator line that weeps behind the unit is the classic slow one, and it can run for months before the floor tells you.

What you see and what it means

  • A musty smell near the washer, dishwasher or fridge usually means moisture has been sitting for a while.
  • Floor planks that cup or lift along the edge of a unit point to water under the finish.
  • Cabinet sides that swell, flake or darken suggest repeated wetting from above or behind.
  • A soft spot in vinyl or laminate near the dishwasher is a sign the subfloor has soaked.
  • A rising water bill with no change in habits can hint at a running supply line.
  • Ceilings below an upstairs laundry showing stains mean water has already passed through the floor.

The City of Colleyville's Utility Billing office at 817.503.1020 handles accounts, and your water meter can also help. If the dial keeps moving with everything off and the appliance valve is closed, the leak is elsewhere in the house.

Safety steps in the first hour

Appliance leaks involve power and water together

Washers, dishwashers and refrigerators all run on household current. Do not reach behind a unit that is standing in water.

  • Close the small shut-off valve behind or under the appliance. If it will not turn, close the home's main valve.
  • Unplug the unit only when the floor and outlet are dry. If not, switch off the circuit at the breaker.
  • Do not pull a heavy appliance across a wet floor, because it can damage flooring and tip.
  • Mop up surface water and open cabinet doors to let air in.
  • Photograph the area, the hose and any visible damage.

What crews actually do about it

  1. 1

    Trace what is wet

    Meters and an infrared camera pick up water inside cabinets, beneath flooring and along wall bases that look dry on the surface.

  2. 2

    Remove what cannot be saved

    Soaked toe-kicks, swollen particleboard and damaged underlayment are removed so air can reach the structure.

  3. 3

    Lift or open flooring if needed

    When water has gone under planks or tile, panels may be released so drying systems can pull moisture from below.

  4. 4

    Run controlled drying

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the area, and crews return to check readings until the materials are dry.

  5. 5

    Hand off the rebuild

    Replacing cabinets, flooring and finish belongs to water damage repair.

Dispatch does not repair appliances. A licensed technician replaces a hose or valve. Our role is the wet structure the leak left behind, and the work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 document as a reference.

What decides the price of the cleanup

  • How long the leak ran. A short burst is a smaller job than months of weeping.
  • Which materials sit under the appliance, such as tile, hardwood, laminate or carpet.
  • Whether water got into the subfloor, wall base or ceiling below.
  • How much cabinetry is affected and how it is built.
  • The days of equipment needed to reach dry readings.
  • Whether odor or mold has already developed.

Appliance leaks and coverage

Texas Department of Insurance material lists a broken washer hose as an example of sudden damage that policies commonly cover, and treats gradual seepage as something they usually do not. An adjuster will look at how old the damage seems. Report hidden damage as soon as you find it, and hold off on permanent repairs until your insurer has looked.

The difference between sudden and gradual is where appliance claims get tricky. A hose that burst this morning sits very differently from a seep that darkened the floor over a season.

Mistakes to avoid and ways to prevent a repeat

  • Leaving a damp floor to dry itself. The surface dries first while the subfloor stays wet, and mold gets a start.
  • Ignoring a musty smell. It usually means water is still hiding.
  • Skipping the hose check. Look at washer hoses once a year and replace any with bulges, rust or cracks.
  • Running the washer while away. A burst hose floods faster when nobody is there to react.
  • Closing the valve only partly. Shut the washer valves after the last load if you travel.

EPA guidance puts the drying target at 24 to 48 hours. That is a tight window in a laundry room or kitchen full of cabinets, so a slow leak found late should still get a call right away.
