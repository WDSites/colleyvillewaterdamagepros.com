Burst Pipe Insurance Claim in Colleyville: What to Do First

By the Colleyville Water Damage Pros team

A burst pipe is often a covered loss, but how you respond in the first hours affects the claim. This guide follows the Texas Department of Insurance guidance and the steps that protect your records.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

The first hour

  1. Shut off the main water valve. The Texas Department of Insurance says an insurer may deny part of a claim if you do not protect the property, and shutting off the water is the first protective step.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and you can reach it standing on a dry floor.
  3. Take photos and video of the burst pipe, the water and every damaged item before moving anything.
  4. Call a plumber to stop the leak and repair the line.
  5. Call the 24/7 restoration line to extract and start drying.

Then, call your insurer

Report the loss as soon as you can. TDI guidance says to contact your insurer promptly and not to wait for hidden damage to show up. It does not list a numeric deadline, so do not assume one, and read your policy for its own notice requirements.

Ask the adjuster what documentation they want, whether they need to see damaged items before they are discarded and how your policy treats the cost of reaching and repairing the pipe. Keep a log of every call with the date and the name of the person you spoke with.

What Texas guidance says about coverage

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowner and renter policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, a toilet overflow or a broken washing machine hose. Policies usually do not cover gradual leaks, and flood damage needs a separate flood policy. TDI also advises homeowners not to make permanent repairs until the insurer has seen the damage.

That is why temporary steps, such as extraction, drying and tarping, come first, and permanent steps, such as new drywall and flooring, wait. The water mitigation page explains the temporary phase and the water damage repair page explains the permanent one.

Mistakes that complicate claims

  • Throwing away damaged items before photographing them
  • Starting permanent repairs before the insurer sees the loss
  • Waiting days to report the loss
  • Letting wet materials sit and grow mold
  • Accepting a verbal estimate with no written scope

A simple claim log

Open a note on your phone and keep every detail in one place. Write the date and time you discovered the burst, when you shut off the water, who you called and when. Add the claim number as soon as you have it. Record each conversation with the insurer, including the name of the person and what they said.

  • Photos and video of the source and the water, with dates
  • Names and phone numbers of the plumber and the restoration company
  • Copies of invoices, receipts for emergency purchases and hotel stays
  • A list of damaged belongings with age and approximate value
  • Emails and letters from the insurer

Talking to the adjuster

An adjuster inspects the loss and applies your policy. Show the adjuster the photos, the readings from the restoration crew and the plumber's report. Ask questions about what is covered and what is not, and ask for decisions in writing. If you disagree with a decision, you can ask the insurer to explain it in writing and contact the Texas Department of Insurance for help with a complaint.

Be honest and complete. A claim built on an accurate record is easier to resolve than one built on memory.

Emergency purchases and temporary housing

Keep receipts for fans, tarps, plastic sheeting and anything else you buy to limit damage. Many policies reimburse reasonable steps taken to protect the property. If the house is not livable while it dries, ask your insurer early whether additional living expenses apply, and keep hotel and meal receipts together in one folder.
