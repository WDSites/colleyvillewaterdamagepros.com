How carpet gets wet in Colleyville homes

Carpet is three parts, not one. The fiber you see sits on a cushion pad, and the pad sits on tack strips and a subfloor. Water that lands on the surface drops straight through the fiber and soaks the pad like a sponge.

Census figures put the owner-occupied rate in Colleyville at 96.7 percent, so most wet carpet here is in a house where the owner decides what gets repaired. Typical sources are a split supply line, a washer hose, an overflowing toilet or an air conditioner drain pan. Rain that comes through a roof opening or under a door adds to the list after storms.

Carpet that stays wet turns into a moisture reservoir. It releases humidity into the room for days, which can soak drywall bottoms and baseboards even after the original leak is fixed.

Surface dryness alone does not prove the layers underneath are dry.

Water category decides whether carpet stays

The S500 document from ANSI and IICRC groups water into three categories, and that grouping drives most carpet decisions. Category 1 comes from a clean supply source such as a broken line or sink overflow. Category 2 carries some contamination, like washing machine discharge. Category 3 is grossly contaminated, such as sewage or river water.

Clean water that is caught fast often allows drying in place, with the carpet lifted at a corner and air pushed under it. Category 2 water usually means the pad comes out and the carpet is cleaned and dried or replaced. With Category 3 water, both carpet and pad go to the dumpster, since fiber cannot be reliably disinfected after sewage soaks in. For that case see our sewage backup page.

The 24 to 48 hour mold clock

EPA guidance sets a target of 24 to 48 hours to get wet materials dry if you want to hold mold back. Texas A&M AgriLife Extension is stricter and puts the goal at 24 hours for soaked areas. Both sources describe the same pressure, which is that carpet pad stays damp far longer than people expect.

Pad is dense foam with little airflow through it. If it is still wet on day three, mold and odor become real possibilities, and the better move is usually removal. If growth is already visible, read about that work on our mold remediation page.

First-hour safety steps for soaked carpet

Before you step on wet carpet

Water and electricity do not mix, and carpet hides how far the water has spread. Treat every outlet, power strip and floor lamp in the wet area as live until proven otherwise.

  • Stop the source first. Close the fixture valve or the main water shutoff.
  • Switch off the breaker for the wet room if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet. A shop vacuum rated for liquids is the only safe type.
  • Lift furniture legs onto foil or plastic blocks so stain from wood does not bleed into the fiber.
  • Call (817) 623-9267 if the carpet is soaked beyond a small patch or the water source is unclear.

If the water came from the ceiling, stay out from under a bulging or sagging area. A wet ceiling can drop without warning, and the carpet below is often the first thing it hits.

How the crew handles wet carpet

  1. 1

    Find the water

    A moisture meter and thermal camera trace where water traveled, since the wet area is usually larger than the stain.

  2. 2

    Extract

    Crews use weighted extraction tools to pull water from the fiber and, where the pad stays, from the cushion beneath it.

  3. 3

    Decide pad and carpet

    Pad is often cut out and replaced, while the carpet itself may be lifted, cleaned and reinstalled when the water was clean.

  4. 4

    Dry the layers

    Air movers push air under lifted carpet or across the exposed subfloor, and a dehumidifier lowers room humidity.

  5. 5

    Check the subfloor and walls

    Readings on the subfloor, tack strips and wall bases confirm whether anything is still holding moisture.

  6. 6

    Clean and deodorize

    Surface cleaning and odor treatment come after drying, so moisture is not sealed in.

What changes the cost of carpet restoration

  • How large the wet area is and whether it crosses into hallways or closets.
  • The water category, since contaminated water changes what can be kept.
  • How long the carpet stayed wet before anyone started drying.
  • Whether the pad can be dried or has to be removed and replaced.
  • Carpet type and quality, because wool and specialty weaves are handled differently from standard nylon or polyester.
  • Whether the subfloor or baseboards also need attention.
  • How many days of equipment the room needs to reach dry readings.

Does insurance pay for wet carpet?

Per TDI, a typical homeowner or renter policy responds to water that arrives suddenly, like a washer hose that snaps, and usually not to a slow leak or seepage. Floods need their own separate policy. Take photos before anything is lifted, and call the dispatch line to start drying while you contact your insurer.

Mistakes that cost carpet

  • Waiting to see if it dries on its own, while the pad stays wet for days.
  • Running a floor fan across the top only, which dries the fiber and leaves the cushion damp.
  • Spraying fragrance over a musty smell instead of finding the wet layer.
  • Pulling the carpet and leaving the pad behind, then putting it back over damp cushion.
  • Skipping photos before cleanup starts.
