Ceiling Water Damage Repair Cost in Colleyville: What Sets It

By the Colleyville Water Damage Pros team

No honest number exists for ceiling water damage repair until someone reads the moisture and sees the ceiling. This guide explains what drives the cost and how to compare quotes without guessing.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Why there is no single price

Ceiling water damage repair can mean a hand-sized patch or a full room of drywall, insulation and paint. Two stains that look alike from the floor can hide very different amounts of wet material. For that reason, this page lists cost factors instead of dollar amounts. Any number you see online without those factors behind it is a guess.

The factors that move the price

FactorWhy it matters
Area of damageMore square footage means more drywall, texture and paint, and often more drying equipment.
Water categoryClean supply water costs less to handle than gray or black water, which needs removal and disinfection.
Insulation aboveWet insulation has to be removed and replaced, which adds labor.
Drying timeMore days of equipment and monitoring adds to the scope.
Texture and finishPopcorn, knockdown and orange peel each take different tools. Matching color across a room adds paint.
AccessA low second-story ceiling is easier to work than a vaulted ceiling over stairs.
Source repairPlumbing or roofing repairs are separate trades with their own costs.

These are factors, not prices.

What a written scope looks like

A good scope lists each material by room: area of drywall removed and replaced, insulation removed, drying equipment and number of days, primer and paint and any texture matching. When you compare quotes, compare these lines. A quote that is only a total, with no breakdown, is hard to check.

The scope is explained before work starts, and you decide whether to proceed. If the damage is small enough for a single handyman visit, we will tell you.

Drying versus rebuilding: two separate costs

Many homeowners get one number for the whole job and are surprised when it splits. Drying and extraction are one cost. Repair and finishing are another. If you ask for water damage repair before drying is complete, you pay twice when a patch fails. That is why the water mitigation readings come first.

Colleyville homes and finishes

Census figures for 2020 to 2024 show a median value of owner-occupied homes in Colleyville of $784,900 and an owner-occupied rate of 96.7 percent.

Houses in that range often have taller ceilings, custom texture and recessed lighting, and all three change the scope of a ceiling repair. If your ceiling holds can lights, a crew has to check the wiring for moisture and an electrician may need to inspect before power is restored.

Insurance and the cost conversation

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and usually do not cover gradual leaks. It also advises homeowners to protect the property from further damage and to let the insurer see the loss before making permanent repairs. The deductible and the covered portion come from your policy, not from us, and we never promise that a claim will be approved.

Keep photos, readings and the written scope together. They are what your adjuster will ask for. If your home is in Southlake or Euless, the same documentation applies.

Questions to ask any contractor

  • Will you read moisture and show me the readings?
  • Is drying included or separate from the repair?
  • What happens if more damage is found when the ceiling is opened?
  • Will you match the existing texture and paint?
  • Who fixes the source of the leak?

An example scope outline, without prices

To show what a written scope looks like, here is the structure of a typical one for a bedroom ceiling that took water from a bathroom above. The numbers in your own scope will differ, but the lines should look like these.

  • Moisture inspection of the ceiling, walls below and attic space above
  • Removal of a section of ceiling drywall and wet insulation, with disposal
  • Drying equipment, number of units and number of days, with readings recorded
  • New drywall, tape, mud and sanding
  • Texture matched to the existing ceiling
  • Prime and paint the full ceiling to the corners
  • Any light fixture removal and reinstall by an electrician

Red flags in a quote

  • A single total with no line items
  • A price quoted over the phone without any inspection
  • Pressure to sign today
  • No mention of drying or moisture readings
  • A promise that insurance will approve the claim
  • A request for the full payment before any work starts

A good company expects questions and explains its scope. If you cannot get a plain answer on what is included, move on.

Why two quotes for the same ceiling can differ

One company may quote only the visible stain, while another quotes the stain, the wet insulation above it and the paint for the whole ceiling. Neither is wrong, but they are not the same job. Lay the line items side by side and compare scope first, then price.

Also compare what happens if the crew finds more water once the ceiling is open. A good quote says how extra work is approved, so a surprise never turns into a bill you did not agree to.
