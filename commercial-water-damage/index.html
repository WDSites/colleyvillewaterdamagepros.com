What this page covers, and what it does not

Think of this number as a dispatcher, not a contractor. It connects callers with professionals for small commercial and mixed-use spaces, such as a storefront, a small office or a unit with living space above or behind it. It does not run its own crews, and it does not claim specialties it cannot back up.

Colleyville is mostly residential. Census figures show 96.7 percent of local housing units are owner-occupied, so most buildings here are homes and commercial calls are fewer. That is why this page stays practical and does not pretend to offer anything beyond arranging a crew and explaining the process.

How commercial water losses start here

The causes are familiar. A supply line splits, a roof lets rain in, a drain backs up, a water heater fails or an air conditioner drain overflows. National Weather Service Fort Worth reports that large hail, damaging winds and flooding occur nearly every year across the Metroplex, with severe weather peaking in spring.

Commercial spaces add complications that a house does not have. Water may travel through a shared wall into the next unit, drop through a drop ceiling or run along a slab. Who owns the pipe or the roof can be unclear until someone reads the lease.

Safety steps for the first hour

Protect people before property

A wet commercial space can hide electrical hazards, slippery floors and falling ceiling tiles. Clear people from the affected area first and keep customers and staff out until it has been checked.

  • Locate the water shutoff and close it if the source is a supply line. Shutting off the water at the main is also something insurers expect you to do.
  • Shut the breaker for wet zones, but only when the panel is reachable from dry footing.
  • Keep people away from sagging ceilings and wet light fixtures.
  • Move inventory, files and electronics off the floor if it is safe to do so.
  • Call (817) 623-9267 and, if water came through a shared wall, tell the property owner or landlord.

If water smells like sewage or is discolored, do not clean it up yourself. Treat it as contaminated and wait for a crew.

What an owner or manager should do first

  1. 1

    Stop the water

    Close the shutoff for the source. If you cannot find it, call the building owner or the City of Colleyville water emergency line from the city's website.

  2. 2

    Make it safe

    Cut power to wet areas, clear the floor of hazards and block off sagging ceilings.

  3. 3

    Document the loss

    Photograph and video the damage, the source and anything damaged before cleanup begins. Keep a list of items affected.

  4. 4

    Call the dispatch line

    Describe the space, the source and what is wet so the right crew can be arranged.

  5. 5

    Notify the right people

    Contact your insurance broker and the property owner or landlord so both know before work is underway.

Photographs taken before anything is moved are often the best record you will have. A crew will also document readings, which later support a claim.

Working around business hours

Many owners cannot close for days. You can ask for work to be scheduled around your hours, with louder tasks like demolition done when the space is empty. That is a request to the crew that is arranged, and it is not something the dispatch line can promise.

Open drying equipment in a small space can be loud and can block aisles. Tell the dispatcher about customer access, deliveries and any rooms that must stay usable. Sometimes drying one section while keeping another open is possible, and sometimes it is not.

How drying and cleanup usually work

The process follows the same principles used in homes. A crew finds the wet materials with moisture meters and a thermal camera, removes standing water, takes out materials that cannot be dried and then sets air movers and dehumidifiers. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the reference most restoration professionals use for this work.

Commercial finishes vary. Carpet tile, vinyl plank, drywall, acoustic ceiling tile and insulation all react differently, and some are removed while others are dried in place. Readings over several visits show when the space is dry. For a deeper look at the first stage, see emergency water removal.

Documentation for a commercial policy

Commercial insurance policies differ widely, and the terms in your policy decide what is covered. Talk to your broker about what your policy requires. Ask about business interruption, contents, and who must file.

  • Dated photos and clips showing the space before and after cleanup.
  • A written note of when the water was discovered and what was done to stop it.
  • Moisture readings and drying logs kept by the crew.
  • Lists of damaged inventory, equipment and furnishings.
  • Copies of your lease sections on repairs and utilities.

Commercial policies are not all alike

Whether a water loss is covered depends on the policy, the cause and who holds it. TDI points out that flood needs its own policy on the home side, so ask your broker whether the same gap exists in a business policy. Your broker can tell you what is covered and what must be reported.

Landlord and tenant questions

Who pays for what depends on the lease. Roofs, plumbing inside walls and shared systems are often the owner's responsibility, while contents and tenant improvements may be the tenant's. That is not a rule, and leases can say the opposite.

The dispatch line does not decide responsibility. Read the lease, call the property owner or landlord, and talk to your broker before approving large work. If a permit is needed for the rebuild, the City of Colleyville's development staff can say which department handles it.

What changes the cost

  • Square footage of the wet area and how far water traveled.
  • The source and whether the water was clean or contaminated.
  • Materials involved, such as carpet tile, drywall or ceiling grid.
  • Whether work must be done at night or in phases around operations.
  • How long the space stayed wet before drying began.
  • Days of equipment and the number of monitoring visits.

Avoidable mistakes

  • Letting cleanup start before photos are taken.
  • Assuming the lease or the policy covers something without reading it.
  • Ignoring a wall that feels dry while the cavity behind it is wet.
  • Discarding damaged items before the insurer has a chance to see them.
  • Waiting for office hours when water is still running.

Floodplain questions belong with the city. the city's Engineering Division oversees development inside floodplains, and the FEMA flood map can be checked at msc.fema.gov. For storm flooding, see flood damage restoration. We also take calls from Grapevine and Euless.
