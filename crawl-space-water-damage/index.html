A plain note on crawl spaces in Colleyville

No source we can cite says how many Colleyville homes sit over a crawl space. Slab foundations are the common case in this area, so most local water problems happen on a concrete floor, and our pages on water damage repair and water mitigation cover that situation.

This page is for the homes that do have a pier and beam foundation, an addition built over a crawl space or a raised section of the house. If that is your house, the rules change. Water can sit beneath the floor for weeks without a single sign upstairs.

How water gets under a raised floor

A crawl space is a low, dark area with bare ground or a thin barrier, and it collects water from several directions at once. Fort Worth's National Weather Service office lists 37.01 inches as DFW's normal rain total for a year, with the heaviest falls in spring and autumn. A downpour can send runoff toward the foundation and through low vents.

  • Plumbing leaks. Supply lines, drain lines and water heater connections often run under the floor, and a pinhole leak can drip for months.
  • Surface drainage. Gutters that dump at the wall, bad grading and clogged downspouts send water toward the vents.
  • Humidity. Warm, damp air enters through vents in summer and condenses on cool ducts, pipes and framing.
  • Storm intrusion. Heavy rain can pool beneath the house when the ground is already saturated.
  • Appliance and AC drains. A condensate line that ends under the house puts a steady trickle into the soil.

Safety steps when water sits under the floor

Do not crawl into standing water

A crawl space combines low clearance, wiring, damp soil and sometimes water. Wiring and a wet crawl space are a dangerous mix, so the first rule is to keep out until the power is dealt with.

  • If a pipe is the source, shut off the water at the main valve or the meter.
  • If water is deep enough to reach wiring, ducts or a water heater, turn off power from the breaker panel first.
  • Do not enter if you smell gas, see sparks or the water looks dark and foul.
  • Watch for snakes, insects and sharp debris if you do look in with a flashlight.

For an after-hours water main or service line emergency, the City of Colleyville says to call the on-call Public Works number, 817.743.4522. For a leak on your own side of the meter, call us and we arrange the crew. Be aware that an insurer may turn down a claim when the owner left the water running instead of closing the main.

How crews dry a crawl space and the floor above it

  1. 1

    Find the source

    Crews trace whether the water comes from a pipe, a drain or the ground, because drying a space with an active leak is wasted effort.

  2. 2

    Remove standing water

    Pumps and wet vacuums clear pooled water. The emergency water removal page covers that step in detail.

  3. 3

    Read the moisture

    Meters check joists, subfloor and sill plates, and thermal imaging helps spot wet insulation without tearing it down.

  4. 4

    Pull what is saturated

    Wet fiberglass batts that sag away from the floor hold water against the wood and usually have to be removed and replaced.

  5. 5

    Dry with air and dehumidifiers

    Air movers push air through the space and dehumidifiers pull moisture out, with readings logged on return visits.

  6. 6

    Check the vapor barrier

    Torn or missing plastic over the soil lets ground moisture keep rising, so crews note it for the repair plan.

Dehumidification is the part many homeowners miss. A crawl space has little airflow, so drying relies on conditioned air control and measured readings. ANSI/IICRC S500 works as the reference point for structural drying, and crews follow its approach to documenting readings rather than judging by touch.

Mold, humidity and the wood that holds up your house

The EPA wants soaked materials dry inside one to two days and indoor humidity under 60 percent, ideally 30 to 50. Holding that range is hard in a crawl space because it is enclosed and shaded. Wood framing that stays wet can grow mold and, over time, weaken.

If growth is already visible on joists or the subfloor, drying alone is not the whole answer. State rules keep the mold assessor and the remediator as two separate licensees on a single project. See mold remediation for that process.

What changes the cost of crawl space work

  • How much water is present and how long it has been there.
  • Clearance, since a very low space slows crews and limits equipment.
  • Whether insulation, vapor barrier or flooring above must be replaced.
  • Whether the source is a plumbing leak, drainage or humidity.
  • How much damage reached the framing and subfloor.
  • Repairs after drying, such as drainage corrections or barrier replacement, which are separate from the dry-out itself.

A dispatcher cannot price this over the phone. A crew has to look at the space first. For permit questions on structural or plumbing repairs, the city's Building Inspections office is the place to ask.

Does insurance pay for crawl space water?

A pipe that split last night and a drip that ran for a year are treated very differently. Texas insurance regulators note that a typical policy answers a sudden burst but not slow seepage, and that ordinary home policies leave out floods entirely. Document what you find and call your insurer early. Nothing permanent should be built before they have looked.

Mistakes and prevention for raised foundations

  • Sealing the vents without a plan, which can trap damp air instead of fixing the cause.
  • Leaving torn vapor barrier on the ground for years.
  • Pointing downspouts at the foundation wall.
  • Ignoring a musty smell because the floor looks fine from above.
  • Waiting for the next dry spell when the space is already wet.
