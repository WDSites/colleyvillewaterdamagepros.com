Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage in Colleyville, TX

Ice usually splits a pipe quietly, and the flood begins when the temperature climbs. If a thawed line is leaking, shut off the main and call. For splits unrelated to cold, see burst pipe water damage.

Why damage appears after the thaw, not during the freeze

Water expands when it turns to ice. Inside a closed pipe, that growth builds pressure between the ice plug and the nearest closed valve, and the pipe wall gives way at its weakest point. The ice itself holds the crack shut, so nothing drips while it stays frozen.

Once the temperature rises, the plug melts and the split opens. Water that was blocked for a day or two now runs at full line pressure through a crack you may never have seen. People often leave for work in a freeze and come home to a flooded floor on a warm afternoon.

North Texas does not freeze often, which is part of the problem. Many homes here were built with plumbing in places that rarely matter, and few owners think about insulation until a forecast shows single digits.

What February 2021 showed this area

At DFW the thermometer bottomed out at minus 2 on February 16, 2021, according to National Weather Service Fort Worth records. The freezing spell lasted 139 hours, starting at 5 pm on February 13 and ending at noon on February 19. Pipes sat in ice for nearly six days and then thawed together.

Public Works lists roughly 209 miles of water mains in the city system. Those mains sit on the city's side of the meter. What froze in most houses were lines on the owner's side, in exterior walls, attics and garages, and the damage came when the whole region thawed at once.

Which pipes are exposed in North Texas homes

  • Supply lines in exterior walls, especially on the north side, where little insulation sits between the pipe and outdoor air.
  • Attic plumbing and water heater lines that run through unconditioned space.
  • Garage supply lines, washer boxes and utility sink plumbing.
  • Outdoor hose bibs and irrigation backflow assemblies.
  • Lines in kitchen and bathroom cabinets on outside walls, where cold air reaches the cabinet interior.
  • Pool equipment lines and sprinkler supply piping.

Plastic and copper both crack. Cross-linked polyethylene tolerates more expansion than copper, but fittings and valves can still fail. Do not assume that a newer house is safe.

First-hour safety steps when a thawed pipe leaks

Before you touch anything wet

Treat any room with water near outlets, fixtures or a ceiling light as electrically unsafe. Stay out until power to that area is off.

  • Close the main water shut-off valve. If it is frozen or stuck, do not force it with a tool that could snap the stem.
  • Switch off power to affected rooms at the breaker only if you can reach it on dry floor.
  • Open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain remaining water from the lines.
  • Do not use an open flame, torch or heat gun on a frozen pipe.
  • Photograph the damage and call (817) 623-9267.

A pipe that is frozen and has not split yet can be warmed slowly with a hair dryer on low, and by opening the cabinet doors. Keep the faucet slightly open so melting water has somewhere to go. If you see any crack or bulge, close the main first.

How crews handle a freeze loss

  1. 1

    Find where the water went

    Frozen pipe splits often hide in attics and walls. A thermal camera and moisture meter show how far the water traveled.

  2. 2

    Extract and remove wet materials

    Water is pumped off the floor and soaked insulation, ceiling drywall and carpet pad are removed. See emergency water removal for how extraction works.

  3. 3

    Dry the structure

    Drying equipment stays in place until the readings fall to the dry goal. Attic spaces and wall cavities get extra attention after a freeze.

  4. 4

    Document the loss

    Moisture readings, photos and notes help the insurer and the repair contractor.

  5. 5

    Hand off to repair

    After drying, water damage repair rebuilds what had to be removed, and a plumber fixes the lines.

The reference for the work is ANSI/IICRC S500. It is a standard that restoration professionals use as a guide, and we cite it as a reference only. During a regional freeze the demand is high, so call early and expect that crews may be busy.

What affects the cost of a freeze loss

  • How many splits there were and how long the water ran after the thaw.
  • Whether the attic, wall cavities and floors below all got wet.
  • The type of insulation and ceiling finish that was soaked.
  • How much of the house shares a duct system or open floor plan, which spreads moisture.
  • The length of the drying period and the equipment needed.
  • How much rebuilding follows drying.

Freeze damage and coverage

As TDI describes it, a pipe that bursts suddenly is the kind of loss standard home policies tend to pick up, while seepage over time is not. A freeze split that floods the house usually reads as sudden, though your own policy wording governs. Take photos, stop the water, and ask your insurer before permanent repairs.

Prevention before the next hard freeze

  • Insulate exposed lines. Foam sleeves on attic, garage and exterior wall pipes cost little.
  • Cover or drain outdoor spigots. Disconnect hoses and shut the interior valve to hose bibs if the house has one.
  • Let faucets drip. A thin steady stream keeps water moving on the coldest nights.
  • Open cabinet doors. Warm room air reaches pipes on outside walls.
  • Know your main valve. Test it before winter and tag it so any adult in the house can find it.
  • Seal air leaks. Cracks around dryer vents and pipe penetrations let freezing air reach plumbing.

Do these when the forecast first shows a hard freeze, not after the pipes are already cold. An hour of work on a mild afternoon is far easier than a flooded house in the dark.
