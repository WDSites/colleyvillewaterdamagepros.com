How Much Does Mold Removal Cost? A Colleyville, TX Guide

By the Colleyville Water Damage Pros team

A mold removal price depends on how much is affected, how it is contained and what has to be rebuilt. This guide explains the factors, the separate bills Texas rules create and how to compare quotes.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Why a flat price does not exist

Mold removal means different things in different houses. It can be cleaning a bathroom ceiling after a fan fix or removing walls, insulation and flooring across several rooms. A company that quotes before it sees the property is guessing, and a company that quotes the same price for every house is not describing yours.

What sets the price

FactorHow it affects the job
Size of the affected areaMore square footage means more labor, materials and disposal.
MaterialsDrywall, insulation and carpet are usually removed. Framing is cleaned.
ContainmentPlastic barriers, negative air and filtration protect the rest of the house.

Two bills, not one: assessment and remediation

Under Texas rules, a licensee cannot perform both the mold assessment and the remediation on the same project, and clearance requires a post-remediation assessment. That means a typical project involves two companies and two invoices, one for the assessment and clearance and one for the removal.

It also means the assessor has no financial stake in how much is removed. The mold remediation page lists the questions to ask each company.

Insurance and mold in Texas

The Texas Department of Insurance says most home policies do not include mold cleanup and testing after a damaged item is removed, though mold on a covered damaged item can be covered. It also says mold from a flood or from a gradual leak is not covered.

The department advises homeowners to protect the property from further damage, to let the insurer see the loss before permanent repairs and to use a state-licensed mold professional who provides a certificate of removal. Read your policy and call your insurer before you decide what to remove.

How to compare quotes

  • Is the assessment and clearance separate from removal?
  • What containment and air filtration are included?
  • Which materials will be removed and which cleaned?
  • Does the quote address the moisture source?
  • Is rebuilding included or separate?
  • Will you get a written protocol before work starts?

Compare the scope, not only the total. A lower total that leaves out containment or clearance is not cheaper in practice. If the source was a recent water loss, the water mitigation page covers drying, and the water damage repair page covers the rebuild.

Ways to keep the cost down

  • Fix the leak and dry the area quickly, because small growth costs less to handle.
  • Do not disturb the growth, because spreading it enlarges the job.
  • Keep photos and the date of the leak for your insurer.
  • Ask for an itemized scope so you can see what each part costs.
  • Control indoor humidity afterward. The EPA says to keep it below 60 percent and ideally between 30 and 50 percent.

Where the money goes in a typical project

A mold project has four phases, and each carries its own labor. The first is assessment and protocol. The second is setup, which includes containment, negative air and protective equipment. The third is the work itself: removing affected materials, cleaning surfaces and drying. The fourth is clearance and rebuild.

Containment and setup are often a surprise to homeowners, because it looks like preparation and not the real job. But the barriers and filtered air are what keep the spores out of your living space, so skipping them to save money defeats the purpose.

Selling or refinancing with mold in the picture

If a buyer's inspection finds mold, a lender or buyer may ask for proof that it was removed. The documents that usually matter are the assessor's protocol, the remediation record and the clearance report. Requirements vary, and we cannot promise what a lender will accept.

Ask your agent early. Removal and clearance take time, and a closing date does not move because the paperwork is not ready.

Ways a mold bill grows

  • The moisture source was never fixed, so growth returns
  • Hidden growth is found behind a wall once it is opened
  • Failed clearance requires a second round of cleaning
  • Materials such as insulation or carpet need replacing
  • The area is large enough to need full containment and negative air

Ask the company how it handles each of these and whether the quote covers a retest. A written answer now saves an argument later.
