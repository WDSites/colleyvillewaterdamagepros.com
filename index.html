Flood restoration company

What a good flood restoration response looks like

Searching for a flood restoration company usually happens at the worst time, with water already on the floor. Speed matters because wet drywall, insulation and carpet pad stay wet for days unless they are opened up and dried. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to head off mold growth, and that window is the reason the dispatch line never closes.

A proper response has four parts. The crew finds and stops the source, extracts standing water, measures how far moisture has traveled with a meter or thermal camera, and then sets air movers and dehumidifiers to dry the structure. Anything that cannot be dried, such as soaked pad or saturated insulation, is removed so it does not hold moisture against framing.

Water is also sorted by how clean it was when it started. A supply line is clean at the source, a washing machine drain or overflow is gray, and sewage or floodwater from outside is black and needs protective gear and removal rather than drying in place. The flood damage restoration page explains the three categories and what each one means for your carpet and walls.

If the loss is a few hours old and the water is still spreading, start with emergency water removal. If the water is stopped and the question is what happens next, water mitigation covers the drying phase in plain language.

Water damage in Colleyville: what the weather and the city utilities mean for you

Dallas-Fort Worth averages 37.01 inches of rain a year over the 1991 to 2020 period, according to the National Weather Service. The same NWS summary notes that large hail, damaging wind and flooding reach the Metroplex nearly every year, with severe weather peaking in spring. For a Colleyville homeowner, that pattern means roof and window leaks after a storm are as likely a source of ceiling stains as a failed pipe.

The city runs its own stormwater program. Its Stormwater Quantity page says flash flooding generally begins at about one inch of rain per hour, that storm inlets are inspected twice a year and that the city has floodgates at six locations. Floodplain permits are handled through the Engineering Division in Public Works, so ask the city about your lot before you assume a flood risk one way or the other.

Cold snaps matter here too. The NWS recorded a low of minus 2 degrees at DFW on February 16, 2021, and temperatures stayed at or below freezing for 139 hours. Pipes in attics and exterior walls were the weak point across North Texas that week, and the same weak points burst in any hard freeze.

Insurance

Water damage and your homeowners policy

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a broken washer hose, while gradual leaks and seepage usually are not covered. Standard home policies also do not cover flood, which needs a separate policy. Your own policy decides, so read it or ask your insurer.

TDI also advises protecting the property from further damage and not making permanent repairs until the insurer has seen the loss. That is why our crews take photos and moisture readings and write down what was removed, so you have a record to hand your adjuster.

Ask before you assume

Coverage depends on your policy and your insurer's decision. We document the loss in writing and we never promise that a claim will be approved.
