Mold Remediation in Colleyville, TX

Mold needs moisture, so mold remediation always starts with the leak. The work covers finding the water source, containing the area, removing affected materials and confirming the result, following the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation.

A musty smell, dark spots in a closet or a stain on the ceiling

Most people do not search for mold remediation first. They notice a smell in a closet, spots on the corner of a bathroom ceiling or a wall that stays damp after a leak. These are the signs that bring callers here, and they usually point to the same cause: water that sat somewhere long enough for mold to take hold.

The first question is not what to spray. It is where the water came from. A dripping toilet supply, a clogged air conditioner condensate line, a roof leak, a shower seal or a window can all keep a surface wet. Until that source is fixed, any cleaning is temporary. The water mitigation page describes how the drying side works.

What mold remediation involves

  1. 1

    Find the moisture

    A crew locates the source and reads moisture in the surrounding materials.

  2. 2

    Contain the area

    Plastic barriers and negative air keep spores from spreading to other rooms.

  3. 3

    Remove and clean

    Affected materials are removed, and remaining surfaces are cleaned with HEPA vacuuming and damp wiping.

  4. 4

    Confirm and dry

    The area is dried to normal readings and checked before rebuild begins.

ANSI/IICRC S520 is the published standard for professional mold remediation, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. The standard describes containment, air filtration, removal methods and verification, which is why a careful job looks like a construction project and not a quick wipe-down.

Mold removal and black mold cleanup

The word black mold gets used for any dark growth, but the color alone does not tell you what it is. Testing can identify what is present, and the more useful question is how much of the building is affected and how long it has been wet. Removal means taking out porous materials such as drywall, insulation and carpet where growth is established, and cleaning solid framing and hard surfaces.

Do not scrub, bleach or sand the area before an assessment. Disturbing it can spread spores through the house, and that makes the job larger. Health questions about exposure belong with a physician, and we do not make health claims about mold.

Mold after water damage

Texas A&M AgriLife Extension and the EPA both say wet materials should be dried within a day or two, and the EPA puts the window at 24 to 48 hours. When a leak is found late or the house is not dried properly, mold can start in the wall cavity, behind baseboards or under flooring where nobody is looking. A crew that finds mold after a water loss often needs to repair what the drying missed.

That is why a dry reading matters as much as a clean-looking surface. If your house had water damage in the past few weeks and now smells musty, call and mention the date of the leak. The water damage cleanup page explains how wet material is removed in the first days.

Testing, protocol and clearance in Texas

Texas runs a mold program through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, which licenses people and companies who assess and remediate mold. Its FAQ says a licensee cannot perform both the mold assessment and the mold remediation on the same project, and that clearance requires a post-remediation assessment. Rules under the state program also say the written protocol must be given to the client at least one calendar day before remediation starts.

Exemptions depend on the type of property, and single-family houses are treated differently from large rental buildings. Ask any company you consider what license it holds and whether your job falls under the state rules. For independent clearance testing, many homeowners hire a separate assessor who has no stake in the remediation.

Selling a house or closing a loan with mold on the record

Some buyers' lenders will not fund until visible mold is removed and the area is confirmed clean. Requirements vary by lender and by inspector, so we cannot promise what a particular lender will accept. A written protocol, a remediation record and a clearance report from an independent assessor give your agent and lender something concrete to review.

Insurance and mold coverage

The Texas Department of Insurance says mold on a covered damaged item can be covered, but most home policies do not pay for mold testing and cleanup after the damaged item is removed. Mold that results from flood is not covered, and neither is mold from a gradual leak. Its guidance also tells homeowners to use a state-licensed mold professional and get a certificate of removal. Check your policy before you decide anything.

Keeping moisture down afterward

The EPA says controlling indoor moisture is the way to control mold, and suggests keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent and ideally between 30 and 50 percent.
