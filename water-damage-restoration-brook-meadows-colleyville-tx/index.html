Water Damage Cleanup in Brook Meadows, Colleyville, TX

Brook Meadows sits in the southwest part of Colleyville near a small private lake and a greenbelt. If a leak or a wet yard is affecting the house, call (817) 623-9267.

Where Brook Meadows is in Colleyville

Brook Meadows is a Colleyville subdivision southwest of the intersection of Glade Road and Pool Road, according to a 2016 Community Impact profile. The paper estimated about 252 homes, a 1994 build-out, and amenities that include a greenbelt, a park, trails and a small private lake.

State association records list both a Brook Meadows association and a Brook Meadows Lakefront association. We treat the neighborhood as one area for service purposes and do not claim formal boundaries. Call with your street and we will confirm that it falls in our Colleyville coverage.

Large homes, long pipe runs

A separate Community Impact profile put the average Brook Meadows home near 4,632 square feet. In a house that large, a supply line can run a long way between the water heater and the farthest bathroom. A leak may be behind a wall far from where the stain appears.

That is a reason to ask for moisture readings instead of judging by the visible stain. We make no claim about how often any Brook Meadows home has had a leak. The point is that size makes finding the source harder, not that the neighborhood is prone to it.

Water from the yard or the lake edge

Homes beside a greenbelt or a small lake can see runoff move across the lot after hard rain. If water comes in under a door or through a wall, photograph where it entered and call. Ask your homeowners association who maintains the lake and its outlets, because we do not know the arrangement.

Water that came from outside can carry soil and bacteria. Keep children and pets away, avoid wading through it, and do not use a household vacuum. See storm water damage for how rain related cleanup works.

Safe first steps

If the water reached outlets or the electrical panel, stay out and treat the area as live.

  • Shut off the water supply only when you can do it from a dry spot.
  • Do not walk through standing water to reach a breaker.
  • Take photos from the doorway before moving anything.
  • Keep wet electronics and small furniture away from the water if you can lift them safely.

What the work looks like

  1. 1

    Find the wet area

    A thermal camera and moisture meters show where the water went, including rooms you have not noticed.

  2. 2

    Pull out pooled water

    Pumps and wet vacuums take off pooled water. The process is explained on the emergency water removal page.

  3. 3

    Dry and monitor

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run while readings are checked on return visits.

  4. 4

    Decide what stays

    Some drywall, pad or flooring can be dried in place and some has to come out. Readings guide that call.

Documenting the loss

Photos, dates and moisture readings give you a record to share with your insurer. Coverage depends on what caused the damage and on your policy wording. Nothing in a restoration record guarantees payment.
