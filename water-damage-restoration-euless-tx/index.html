Four creeks cross Euless

The city's FY 2023 to 2024 budget says four major creeks cross Euless: Little Bear Creek, Hurricane Creek, Boyd Branch and Blessing Branch. A creek maintenance project clears fallen trees and debris from bridge crossings, which tells you what the city worries about when a storm hits. A USGS gauge on Big Bear Creek at State Highway 121 in Euless reports a drainage area of 34.8 square miles.

Creeks carry runoff after heavy rain, and they rise fast. The city's Villages of Bear Creek Park, at 1951 Bear Creek Parkway, is described on its own page as land in the Bear Creek floodplain. That is an honest, simple reminder that some of the lowest ground in a neighborhood is the part nature designed to flood.

Creek-side and street-level: where Euless water damage starts

Homes close to a creek or drainage easement can see water at garage doors, patios and low windows after a downpour. Homes farther away see leaks from roofs, water heaters and supply lines. Both end up on the same line, and the flood damage restoration page covers storm water while the water mitigation page covers drying after any source.

Water supply, wells and permits in Euless

The city says its primary water comes from the Trinity River Authority, which draws on the Cedar Creek and Richland Chambers reservoirs by way of Lake Arlington. It also uses city wells in the Trinity Aquifer as a secondary source. The Water and Wastewater division is reachable at (817) 685-1580, and Water Utilities handles billing at (817) 685-1471.

The city's Building Inspections page says permits are needed for water heaters, AC condensers, plumbing changes and structural repairs, and that re-roofing does not need one.
