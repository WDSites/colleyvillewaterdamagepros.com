Neighborhoods, channels and creeks in North Richland Hills

North Richland Hills counted 69,917 residents in the 2020 census, the largest of the nearby towns we serve. The city's own budget documents name Big Fossil Creek, Calloway Branch near the Graham Ranch subdivision and Mackey Creek, and they describe flood studies near the Meadow Lakes subdivision and a drainage project for Emerald Lakes. The city also reports seven miles of concrete-lined channels.

The city's Storm Water Drainage page says runoff flows to creeks and channels and ultimately to the Trinity River, that heavy rain can overwhelm the system and that some homeowners live inside a floodplain. The city also has a Know Your Flood Risk page for checking an address. A house backing onto a concrete channel behaves differently from one on a hill, and that difference shows up in what kind of water damage you are likely to see.

Water heaters, HVAC and permits

The city's Building Inspections page says permits are required for water heater, HVAC, electrical and gas work. That matters after a loss because a flooded garage or a burst water heater often leads to replacement equipment, and the replacement needs a permit like any other install. Ask your plumber or HVAC contractor who pulls it before work begins.

The city's Water Department is at City Hall at 4301 City Point Drive, and Public Works Utility Division handles water and sewer lines. The city buys sewer treatment from the City of Fort Worth and the Trinity River Authority, so a sewer problem on a city line goes to the city, while a line inside your property goes to a plumber.
