Southlake homes and why the drying plan matters

The city's own 2025 profile puts Southlake's median home sales price at $1,652,368. Houses at that level often carry hardwood, stone, custom cabinetry and built-in millwork, and those finishes are costly to replace and hard to match. Fast, monitored drying is the way to keep as many of them as the readings allow.

A good plan names the target moisture levels in advance, places air movers and dehumidifiers to cover cabinets and subfloor, and records daily readings. It also leaves a written trail for your insurer. The water mitigation page explains each step, and the water damage repair page covers rebuilding.

Ponds, channels and creeks: who is responsible in Southlake

Southlake's stormwater program is organized around two drainage basins, Denton Creek and Big Bear Creek, and the city also names Dove Creek and Timarron Creek in its stormwater documents. Its city page says runoff flows toward Lake Grapevine and Big Bear Creek and that flash flooding can develop within minutes.

The city maintains pipes, culverts and bridges on public roads. Its stormwater page says property owners and homeowners associations maintain private ponds, channels and creeks. That detail matters after a storm. If water backs up from a retention pond or channel behind your home, the first call may be to your HOA or the property owner, and the cleanup call is to us.

The city's Floodplain page explains the FEMA map basics and notes that standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood. Review it before you decide how much flood protection to carry.

A city that runs on Fort Worth water

Southlake buys all of its water from the City of Fort Worth and distributes it itself. The city's 2022 water quality report notes that during the February 2021 winter storm, Fort Worth could not supply water and Southlake connected to Grapevine's system for about five and a half hours. That was a supply problem. It is also a reminder that pressure drops and thaw-and-refreeze cycles can strain pipes inside a house.

For water emergencies on city property, Public Works Administration lists an emergency line at (817) 743-4522. Leaks inside the house belong on the restoration line.
