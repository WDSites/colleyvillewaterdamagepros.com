My wall is wet and the carpet is soaked: is that water mitigation?

Yes. People rarely search for the trade name when water is spreading. They type that the wall is damp, the carpet squishes or a ceiling stain keeps growing. Water mitigation is the name for the work that answers those problems: stop the source, remove what is wet beyond saving and dry the rest before it breaks down.

Mitigation is not the rebuild. It comes first, and its goal is to keep the loss small enough that water damage repair later means fewer materials and less cost. If standing water is still present, begin with emergency water removal and then move to drying.

When water mitigation becomes an emergency

Any leak that is still running or any structure that is soaking after the water stops is time-sensitive. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and Texas A&M AgriLife Extension gives a similar guideline. That window is why we answer at night and on weekends.

If it is after hours, call anyway. A dispatcher can tell you to shut off the main valve, what to unplug and what to move, and a crew can be sent the same day when one is available. Nothing about drying works faster if you wait for office hours.

What happens in the first 72 hours

  1. 1

    Stop the source

    The main valve is shut, and the leak is identified so it does not restart.

  2. 2

    Map the moisture

    Moisture meters and a thermal camera show wet areas that look dry from the surface.

  3. 3

    Remove and extract

    Standing water goes first, then wet carpet pad, insulation and drywall that cannot be dried.

  4. 4

    Set the equipment

    Air movers and dehumidifiers start the drying. Readings are recorded each visit.

The equipment, in plain words

  • Moisture meters read how wet drywall, wood and subfloor are, which a hand cannot tell.
  • Thermal imaging cameras show cool, wet areas behind walls and under floors.
  • Air movers are the low, loud fans that push air across wet surfaces to evaporate moisture.
  • LGR dehumidifiers pull the evaporated water out of the air so it does not settle back into the house.
  • Desiccant dehumidifiers are used in some conditions where a standard unit cannot reach the target.

The goal is a dry standard. A crew sets drying goals from the readings on unaffected materials in your own house and checks daily until the wet materials match them. Equipment that comes off too early leaves moisture in framing, and that is how mold appears a few weeks later.

People sometimes ask about renting air movers and dehumidifiers. Drying works only when the equipment is placed and monitored correctly, so this site does not offer equipment rental. The crew handles placement, daily readings and pickup.

Structural drying: how long it takes

Structural drying usually takes several days, and the number depends on the water category, the materials and the humidity in the house. Hardwood and subfloor dry slowly. Drywall dries faster, and concrete slab dries slowest of all. The crew checks and records moisture every visit, so the end date is based on readings and not a guess.

Documentation that helps your claim

Each visit records moisture readings, equipment placement, photos and a list of what was removed. The Texas Department of Insurance advises homeowners to protect the property from further damage and to let the insurer see the loss before permanent repairs begin. That written trail is what you hand your adjuster, and it makes the claim conversation about facts instead of memory.

Coverage itself comes from your policy. TDI says most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, but gradual leaks and flood are usually excluded. We never promise that a claim will be approved.
